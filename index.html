The Problem

Every operations team already has dashboards. None of them can defend a decision.

Signals sit in six systems, the number on the slide cannot be traced back to a record, and nobody wrote down what was decided or whether it worked. Six months later the call has to be justified from memory.

Fragmented data

Customer signals are scattered across ticketing, telephony, QA and survey tools — nobody sees the full picture.

Untraceable numbers

A figure appears on a dashboard with no path back to the records that produced it — so it can be shown, but never defended.

Manual analysis

Hours in spreadsheets produce error-prone, inconsistent answers that are stale the moment they're finished.

No decision record

Tools recommend and then forget. Nothing records what was actually decided, on what evidence, or whether it moved the metric.

The Platform

One governed loop from signal to decision to measured outcome.

Not four tools that integrate. One loop, where each stage is constrained by the one before it — and the last stage feeds back so the platform can be shown to have been wrong.

Detection is arithmetic

Nine detectors compare a recent window against your own baseline. Nothing is raised until it clears a declared threshold, so a quiet week produces an empty feed instead of invented findings.

Cause is labelled a hypothesis

Attribution is by decomposition — contributions are shares of a measured delta and sum to 100%. The system never claims confirmed cause; a human closes that gap.

Some exposures are not priced

Compliance risk returns no dollar figure at all. A fabricated fine probability would be the least defensible number in the product, so it declines to produce one.

Decisions are recorded, not just recommended

Committing an action snapshots the evidence as it stood. The loop closes when the same signal is re-read and the outcome is reported — including when it got worse.

Decision Ledger

A recommendation is not a decision. A decision is a human committing to an action, on stated evidence, at a stated confidence. The ledger records that commitment — the severity, the confidence and the metric as they stood at that moment — then re-reads the same signal on every engine pass and reports what happened.

Because the evidence is snapshotted and never back-dated, the platform can be shown to have been wrong. That is the property an auditor is actually looking for, and it is the reason this is decision intelligence rather than reporting.

The Decision Cycle

Four steps, and the fourth is the one nobody else does.

Most tools stop at step two. The value is in steps three and four — committing to an action on the record, and being told honestly whether it worked.

Commit the decision

Take the action on the record. The ledger snapshots the severity, the confidence and the metric exactly as they stood at that moment — and flags it if you committed below the confidence floor.

Get told whether it worked

The same signal is re-read on every engine pass and scored against the snapshot: cleared, improving, holding, or worse than when you started. Including when the answer is embarrassing.
